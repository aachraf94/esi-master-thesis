\uchapter{Abstract}

Classical data warehouse architectures impose rigid structural and analytical constraints
that limit the scalability of decision support information systems (DSIS): they cannot
absorb schema changes and new source integrations without disruptive re-engineering, and
they relegate predictive analytics to peripheral add-ons. Extending them incrementally
reproduces the problem at larger scale.

This thesis frames the core challenge as a \textit{dual-scalability deficit} —
\textit{structural scalability} (absorbing schema evolution and source heterogeneity without
re-engineering) and \textit{analytical scalability} (embedding predictive and prescriptive
models natively in the decision pipeline) — and examines whether Data Vault~2.0 and the
Lakehouse paradigm can be composed to close it. The central argument is that composition
is achievable, but conditioned on an explicit integration contract that reconciles
DV2.0's hash-keyed relational lineage discipline with the Lakehouse's schema-on-read file
layer. Three conceptual contributions operationalize this finding: a formal dual-scalability
framework (C1), a composability analysis of Data Vault~2.0 and the Lakehouse with a
six-criteria evaluation instrument (C2), and a layered reference architecture with a formal
integration contract evaluated against the criteria (C3).

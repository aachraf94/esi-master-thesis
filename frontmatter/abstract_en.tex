\uchapter{Abstract}

Classical Decision Support Information Systems (DSIS) rely on data warehouse architectures
designed for stable business environments and fixed analytical needs. Faced with accelerating
organizational transformations, the proliferation of heterogeneous data sources, and the
growing analytical maturity of organizations, these rigid architectures become an obstacle
to the evolution of decision support systems.

This research addresses the problem of scalability in decision-making architectures along
two complementary axes: \textit{structural scalability}, which concerns the system's ability
to integrate new sources, manage schema changes, and adapt to volume growth without
disrupting the existing architecture; and \textit{analytical scalability}, which reflects
the progression from purely descriptive BI toward predictive and prescriptive capabilities
natively integrated into the decision pipeline.

To address these challenges, this study relies on the Data Vault~2.0 methodology as the
foundation of the agile modeling layer, and explores patterns for integrating advanced
analytical models within modern architectures (Lakehouse, Delta Architecture). The thesis
delivers three conceptual contributions: a formal dual-scalability framework (C1), a
critical composability analysis of Data Vault~2.0 and the Lakehouse paradigm with a
criteria instrument (C2), and a layered reference architecture with a formal integration
contract evaluated against six criteria (C3).

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Scalable architecture, Decision support information system,
Data Vault~2.0, Agile data warehouse, Predictive analytics, Business Intelligence,
Lakehouse, ETL/ELT, Schema evolution, Machine learning.

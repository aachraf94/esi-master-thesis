## DESCRIPTION OF THE END-OF-STUDIES PROJECT

**Abstract:**  
Classical Decision Support Information Systems (DSIS) rely on data warehouse architectures designed for stable business environments and fixed analytical needs. Faced with accelerating organizational transformations, the proliferation of heterogeneous data sources, and the growing analytical maturity of organizations, these rigid architectures become an obstacle to the evolution of decision support systems.

This research addresses the problem of scalability in decision-making architectures along two complementary axes: structural evolution, which concerns the system's ability to integrate new sources, manage schema changes, and adapt to volume growth without disrupting the existing architecture; and analytical evolution, which reflects the progression from purely descriptive BI toward predictive and prescriptive capabilities natively integrated into the decision pipeline.

To address these challenges, this study relies on the Data Vault 2.0 methodology as the foundation of the agile modeling layer, and explores patterns for integrating advanced analytical models (machine learning, predictive analytics) within modern architectures (Lakehouse, Delta Architecture).

**Keywords:**  
Scalable architecture, Decision support information system, Data Vault 2.0, Agile data warehouse, Predictive analytics, Business Intelligence, Lakehouse, ETL/ELT, Schema evolution, Decision-making machine learning

**Objectives:**  
- Conduct a structured literature review on data warehouse modeling approaches (Kimball, Inmon, Data Vault 2.0) and modern decision-making architectures (Lambda, Kappa, Lakehouse).  
- Analyze the limitations of classical architectures in the face of requirements for structural agility and increasing analytical maturity.  
- Propose a reference architectural framework for scalable DSIS, integrating an agile modeling layer (Data Vault 2.0) and a native predictive analytics layer.  
- Conduct a comparative study of modeling approaches based on defined criteria: flexibility, query performance, schema evolution support, implementation complexity, and analytical capability.

**Expected Outcomes:**

\chapter{Dual Scalability: Existing Architectures and Approaches}

\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

The two preceding chapters reached complementary verdicts. Chapter~1 found that Data Vault~2.0 best addresses structural scalability but stops at descriptive analytics. Chapter~2 found that the Lakehouse, implemented through the Delta architecture, best addresses analytical scalability but depends on a modeling discipline it does not supply. Each approach covers the gap the other leaves. This short chapter asks what the literature reports about architectures that address \textit{both} forms of scalability at once. It does not propose a new architecture; it reports what others have built or described.

\section{Defining Dual Scalability}
\label{sec:ch3-dual}

\textit{Dual scalability}, as used here, is the property of an architecture that is structurally and analytically scalable at the same time: it absorbs new sources and schema change additively (Chapter~1) and supports the full analytical maturity spectrum natively (Chapter~2). The two prior verdicts make the combination an obvious candidate rather than a coincidence. Data Vault~2.0 supplies the historization, stable business keys, and governed lineage that the Lakehouse's data-readiness criterion left unfulfilled; the Lakehouse supplies the open storage, workload plurality, and native model loop that Data Vault's analytical ceiling left unreachable. The strengths are near-complementary, which is why the literature and industry have converged on combining them rather than choosing between them \parencite{linstedt2015, armbrust2021}.

\section{What the Literature Reports: Data Vault 2.0 on the Lakehouse}
\label{sec:ch3-existing}

In practice the combination takes one dominant form: Data Vault~2.0 is implemented \emph{on top of} the Lakehouse storage layer, not as an alternative to it \parencite{databricks2022} \textit{(industry source; not peer-reviewed)}. The vault objects are stored as open table-format tables, ingestion uses auto-loading, and loading is orchestrated through declarative pipelines. Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-dv2-on-lakehouse} shows the canonical placement on the medallion layers.

\fig[0.9\textwidth]{dv2-on-lakehouse-databricks.png}{Data Vault~2.0 mapped onto the Lakehouse medallion: staging feeds Bronze, the Raw and Business Vaults occupy Silver, and star-schema marts are served from Gold. Source: \parencite{databricks2022} \textit{(industry/vendor source; not peer-reviewed)}.}{ch3-dv2-on-lakehouse}

The mapping is direct: file staging feeds the Bronze landing zones; the Raw Vault and Business Vault occupy the Silver layer; star-schema marts and point-in-time views are served from Gold. The vault becomes a \emph{tenant} of the Lakehouse rather than a competitor to it. Independent and vendor accounts converge on the same pattern, with several pushing staging and hash-key computation onto the lake before populating the vault \parencite{moermans2023, moronta2026} \textit{(industry sources; not peer-reviewed)}. On the academic side, a peer-reviewed study reports the same direction, modeling data lakes with Data Vault to combine the lake's flexibility with the vault's lineage discipline \parencite{giebler2019dv}. Adjacent governance patterns such as data contracts are also proposed to hold structural discipline over flexible storage \parencite{jones2023datacontracts} \textit{(industry source; not peer-reviewed)}.

Two conclusions follow from the literature, not from any position taken here. First, an architecture addressing both forms of scalability already exists in practice: Data Vault~2.0 on the Lakehouse pairs the strongest structural approach with the strongest analytical one, and the pairing is coherent because the vault becomes a governed tenant of the open store \parencite{databricks2022, giebler2019dv}. Second, the combination rests mostly on industry and vendor sources; peer-reviewed evaluation is thin, and the trade-offs it carries --- the join-heavy query cost the vault imposes on columnar storage, the overlap between satellite historization and table-version time travel, and the added skills burden --- are acknowledged but not systematically measured \parencite{schneider2024, harby2024}. The state of dual scalability is thus one of a practised pattern ahead of its evidence: widely combined by convention, not yet methodically evaluated in the academic record.

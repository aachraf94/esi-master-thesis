\uchapter{General Conclusion}

\section*{Summary of the Study}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Summary of the Study}

This thesis surveyed decision support architectures along two forms of scalability that the literature has largely studied apart. Structural scalability is the capacity to absorb new sources, schema change, and volume growth without re-engineering; analytical scalability is the capacity to support the full maturity spectrum --- descriptive through prescriptive --- natively in the pipeline. Proposing no architecture of its own, the study surveyed the existing approaches on each axis, compared them against criteria drawn from the literature, and reported what already exists at their intersection.

\section*{Findings}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Findings}

The three objectives are answered as follows, each from the surveyed literature.

\noindent\textbf{Structural scalability.} Chapter~1 compared the Inmon Corporate Information Factory, the Kimball dimensional model, Data Vault~2.0, and Anchor modeling against five criteria. The classical paradigms were engineered for a stable schema and absorb change poorly, because the very features that deliver enterprise consistency --- a normalized core, conformed dimensions --- are shared surfaces through which change propagates. The additive family inverts this profile, and Data Vault~2.0 emerged as the approach the literature treats as the practical reference point for structural scalability: it makes schema change additive rather than destructive, at a documented cost in query complexity and implementation effort \parencite{linstedt2015, inmon2005, kimball2013}.

\noindent\textbf{Analytical scalability.} Chapter~2 compared the Lambda and Kappa streaming designs, the data lake, the Lakehouse, and the Delta architecture against five further criteria. The streaming architectures solved freshness but left advanced analytics outside the pipeline; the data lake opened the store to machine learning but could not make its data dependable. The Lakehouse, with its open table formats and the native model loop they enable, was found to support the full maturity spectrum on one governed store, with the Delta (medallion) architecture as its dominant implementation pattern \parencite{armbrust2021, harby2024, schneider2024, databricks2022}.

\noindent\textbf{Dual scalability.} Chapter~3 reported that an architecture addressing both forms already exists in practice: Data Vault~2.0 implemented on top of the Lakehouse, with the vault as a governed tenant of the open store. But the evidence is uneven --- the combination rests mostly on industry and vendor sources, with thin peer-reviewed evaluation and trade-offs that are acknowledged informally rather than measured \parencite{databricks2022, giebler2019dv, schneider2024}.

\section*{Limitations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Limitations}

The study's conclusions inherit the limits of a literature-based method, which it states plainly.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{No measurement.} The comparisons rest on criteria-based assessment grounded in the cited sources, not on benchmarks. The ordinal scores indicate relative standing, not measured performance.
  \item \textbf{Uneven evidence at the intersection.} The dual-scalability finding leans on industry and vendor publications, because the Data Vault~2.0--Lakehouse combination is not yet studied formally in the peer-reviewed literature.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} The survey covers the principal mainstream approaches on each axis; it does not claim to be exhaustive of every modeling variant or analytical platform.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Directions the Literature Points Toward}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Directions the Literature Points Toward}

The gaps identified above mark where further work is needed, and the literature itself points in three directions.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation of the combination.} A benchmark of Data Vault~2.0 on an open table format, measured against the structural and analytical criteria used here, would turn the conventional combination into a measured one and close the evidence gap of Chapter~3.
  \item \textbf{Quantifying the trade-offs.} The join-heavy query cost the vault imposes on columnar storage, and the overlap between satellite historization and table-version time travel, are acknowledged but unmeasured; both are concrete targets for study.
  \item \textbf{Consolidated criteria.} The two criteria instruments used in this thesis are drawn from the literature but not standardized; a validated, theoretically grounded instrument for dual scalability would let future surveys compare architectures on a common footing.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The thesis set out to read decision support architectures for both structural and analytical scalability and to report what the literature concludes. Its central finding is that the field already supplies a strong answer on each axis --- Data Vault~2.0 for structure, the Lakehouse for analytics --- while their combination, though practised, still awaits the peer-reviewed evaluation that would confirm it.
